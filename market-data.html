Uranium prices, production and nuclear capacity, by the numbers

Uranium spot and term prices, physical holdings, miner production, US output and purchases, and the reactor fleet that burns the fuel — from published sources.

Two different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the price of the pound: spot, term, and what utilities actually paid on delivered contracts. The second kind measures the physical market: how much was mined, how much is held, and how many reactors need fuel. The first kind tells a reader what the market thinks. The second tells a reader whether the market can deliver.

What the latest numbers show is a market repricing the future faster than the present: the term price has broken its 2007 ceiling while spot is still a third below its 2007 peak, the largest physical holder keeps buying, and the two largest miners are both producing below plan — while 78 GW of new nuclear capacity is under construction.

How to read these numbers

Included: TradeTech and Cameco spot and term prices to October 2026; Sprott Physical Uranium Trust holdings and purchases; Cameco Q3 2026 production, sales and guidance; Kazatomprom 2025 production and 2026 guidance; EIA US production and marketing figures for 2025; IEA global nuclear capacity and construction; the World Nuclear Association's 2040 demand forecast; and NexGen's Rook I project figures. Excluded: any figure from a paywalled or login-gated price service (UxC and TradeTech primary feeds are not directly quoted — only their published month-end indicators as reported by Cameco, FNArena, and Benchmark); secondary price feeds that could not be tied to a named indicator; and the EIA's unfilled-requirements figure for 2026–2035, which was seen only in secondary summaries and is logged as a gap in SOURCES.md.

Spot and term prices come from different indicator families (TradeTech weekly indicators versus Cameco's month-end average of UxC and TradeTech quotes) and differ by a few dollars; the table labels each figure with its indicator so the two are never blended. Forecasts and planning figures are labeled as forecasts, not actuals.
