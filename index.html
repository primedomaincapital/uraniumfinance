The thesis

A name that states the fuel and the money.

Nuclear power runs on a commodity most energy brands never mention. Uranium is mined in a handful of countries, converted and enriched in fewer still, and bought under contracts that can run a decade. The money moves before the fuel does — and the name that owns that conversation owns the market's front door.

That is why this name works as a business identity rather than a slogan. A miner reading it knows the subject is the capital that funds production. A utility reading it knows the subject is the procurement that keeps reactors running. An investor reading it knows the subject is the price of the pound and the equities that follow it.

The name is short, pronounceable, and exact. It works on a uranium fund's prospectus, on a fuel-procurement desk, on a mining company's investor page, and in front of a fund manager who needs to know, in one glance, that this is not a general energy site. It is a uranium site first, and a finance site second.

Specific, and broad enough to travel. A strong uranium-finance brand must cover three linked markets at once: the physical pound and its price, the companies that mine and enrich it, and the funds and contracts that finance it. This name covers uranium trading, mining finance, nuclear investment research, and fuel-cycle capital without narrowing to any single one — and without vagueness that says nothing at all.
